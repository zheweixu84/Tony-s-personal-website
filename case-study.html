Building My Digital
Design Identity.

From hands-on experiments to an interactive portfolio website. A detailed record of the ideas, making, design decisions, AI-assisted coding and repeated revisions behind my personal online identity.

TONY XUUNIVERSITY OF AUCKLANDSKETCH · MODEL · MAKE2026 / SEMESTER 2

01 / RESEARCH

Looking at
other portfolios.

I began by looking at how different designers introduce themselves online. Sam Scott uses oversized typography and bold contrast to create a strong first impression, while Jimmy Ma presents clear personal information and straightforward navigation.

I wanted to take something from both approaches. The site needed a recognisable visual identity, but visitors should still be able to move through my work and understand my process without unnecessary steps.

What I took forward

Visual identity

One memorable introduction instead of filling the homepage with disconnected graphics.

What I took forward

Clear navigation

A simple route to projects, skills, and personal information.

What I took forward

Content first

Make the finished work and the process behind it equally accessible.

02 / INITIAL DEVELOPMENT

From paper
to an idea.

I first drew rough homepage and project-page sketches rather than spending time polishing the initial layout. They helped me decide where to put personal information, selected projects, navigation and the full archive.

I also explored my identity through physical materials. The phrase Sketch · Model · Make came from the way I normally work, and the connected foam pieces and printed cards gave me a tangible starting point before translating the concept into a digital experience.

03 / WEBSITE PROTOTYPING

Getting it
working first.

The first HTML versions were quite simple. I could move around the site and see an initial structure, but there was a lot of empty space and some of the projects were still represented by placeholders. This made it difficult to understand what I actually designed.

I used AI to help write and modify the HTML, CSS and JavaScript because web development was not my main skill. I tested each version in a browser and gave feedback on layout, navigation, spacing and content. The early working pages helped me spot issues much faster than static sketches.

04 / KEY ITERATION 01

The opening
interaction.

I wanted visitors to experience my process rather than simply read three words. Earlier versions used static or simple panels, but the relationship between the introduction and the rest of the website did not feel convincing.

Through repeated AI-assisted coding and testing, I adjusted the scale, flip motion, panel separation and graphic details. The developed opening connects the three cards to my name and design direction, creating an introduction that better reflects my interest in physical interaction.

05 / KEY ITERATIONS 02 & 03

Identity,
information
and clarity.

I continued changing the logo and the About section after testing them in the real website. The symbol developed from a basic TX mark into a simpler blue-and-white geometric identity, and I removed the extra subtitle because I wanted it to represent me as an individual rather than a studio.

My biography also needed to be more specific about product design, physical prototyping and interactive experiences. I rewrote it to reflect how I work, updated the CV download access, and checked that the website and Behance introduction were consistent.

06 / ORGANISING THE WORK

More than
a homepage.

The portfolio gradually changed from a handful of inconsistent featured cards into a full project archive. I introduced category filters, individual case-study pages and space for more photographs, prototypes and decisions.

Some AI-assisted edits put the wrong images into projects or introduced issues elsewhere on the site. I had to keep checking every cover, gallery image, caption and page interaction. This made me realise that good project presentation depends on careful content organisation as much as visual design.

07 / WORKING WITH AI

Explain.
Test. Correct.
Repeat.

AI was a practical technical collaborator throughout this project. I used it for code, layout experiments, animation implementation and troubleshooting. It made web development more accessible to me, but it was not a one-step process and some results did not match what I had asked for.

I kept giving more specific instructions, running the code, finding mistakes and requesting corrections. I decided which results worked for my goals. The design direction, project selection, revisions and quality checks remained my responsibility.

Check

03 / Iterate

Improve

Correct mistakes, refine the result and test it again.

08 / FINAL OUTCOME

Now it's
live.

The final result is a published personal portfolio that combines my visual identity, an interactive opening, a structured collection of work and a clearer way to contact me. Moving the site onto GitHub Pages also meant checking image paths, video playback, mobile layouts and downloadable files.

Unlike an image mock-up, this is a working website I can continue updating as I make more projects. That ongoing quality of the work is important to me.

09 / PERSONAL REFLECTION

What I learned.

Developing my personal portfolio was more complicated than I expected. At first I was mostly thinking about the overall visual style, but I quickly realised that organising content, building interactions and keeping everything working were just as important. I also learned that a portfolio needs to help other people understand the designer behind the work, not just show a collection of finished images.

One of my strengths is being able to turn ideas into working outcomes quickly. I prefer making something and testing it instead of planning every small detail in advance. This helped me explore several versions of the homepage, opening animation and project pages. However, I did not organise every project image and category clearly enough at the beginning. That created additional work and repeated corrections later.

AI helped me with programming, interaction development and troubleshooting, especially in areas where I had limited experience. It allowed me to test ideas more quickly, but the outputs were not always accurate. Sometimes new changes introduced other problems, so I had to continue explaining what I wanted, finding errors and deciding what to keep. Using AI did not remove my responsibility for the quality of the work. It showed me how important clear communication and independent design judgement are.

My main interest is still in physical product design and hands-on making. Bringing my Sketch · Model · Make idea into a website helped me see how the same working process can move across different media. In future projects I want to improve my planning and asset organisation while keeping the fast testing and experimentation that helped me develop this website.
